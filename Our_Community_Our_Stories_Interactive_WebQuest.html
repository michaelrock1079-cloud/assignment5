Teacher Page

Title: Our Community, Our Stories

Grade Level: 3–5

Subjects: Social Studies, English Language Arts, Technology

Estimated Time: Two 40–45 minute instructional periods

Learning Objectives

  • Identify characteristics that help define a community.
  • Examine similarities and differences among communities.
  • Use digital resources to gather information.
  • Create a multimedia product to communicate information.
  • Demonstrate respect for cultural and individual differences.
  • Reflect on connections between their own experiences and those of others.

Why This Lesson Is Culturally Supportive

This WebQuest was designed around Chisholm’s (1998) six culturally supportive elements for technology integration. Click each element below.

Cultural Awareness

Recognizing differences and similarities.

Cultural Relevance

Connecting learning to student experience.

Supportive Environment

Safe, inclusive, and respectful.

Equitable Access

Multiple ways to access learning.

Instructional Integration

Technology with a clear instructional purpose.

Cultural Relevance & Awareness

Chisholm (1998) emphasizes connecting technology-supported instruction with students’ backgrounds, prior experiences, knowledge, interests, and learning needs. This lesson gives students ownership over what community they investigate and how they communicate what they learn.

Students can draw on families, neighborhoods, interests, experiences, languages, hobbies, cultures, and communities as legitimate sources of knowledge. At the same time, students are not required to act as representatives of a racial, ethnic, religious, or cultural group. They may instead investigate a school, neighborhood, sports team, military community, hobby group, or another meaningful community.

This combination of choice, representation, accessibility, and respectful exploration helps create a culturally supportive learning experience.

Differentiation & Accommodations

  • Speech-to-text and text-to-speech.
  • Recorded responses instead of extensive writing.
  • Drawings instead of photographs.
  • Graphic organizers and sentence starters.
  • Partner work when appropriate.
  • Translated vocabulary or use of a home language.
  • Teacher-selected simplified resources.
  • Printed materials when Internet access is unavailable.
  • Private presentation to the teacher when appropriate.
  • A nondigital alternative when technology creates an accessibility barrier.

Suggested respectful response stems:

  • “One thing I learned was…”
  • “Something our communities have in common is…”
  • “One difference I noticed was…”
  • “I would like to learn more about…”
  • “Thank you for teaching me about…”
